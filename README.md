## What it does

- **Type a word and pick a font.** 50 fonts, from flowing scripts to bold varsity letters, with an optional swoop
  underneath, outlines and shadows.
- **Add an emoji.** Every emoji is ready to stitch, in its own colours.
- **Bring in your own pictures.** Logos, clipart, drawings, even paintings and photos. Fembroider picks the thread
  colours for you, or keeps the colours of an SVG.
- **Three ways to stitch a picture.** *Flat art* for logos, *Painting* for paintings and photos, *Line art* for
  drawings and engravings (examples below).
- **Open designs you already have.** PES, DST, EXP, JEF, VP3, HUS, XXX and PEC files, or a whole zip of them as
  downloaded. Move them, recolour them, leave colours out, and add a name before saving for your machine.
- **Fine-tune any part by clicking it.** Click a shape in the preview and a round menu opens right there: sew
  it as lines or an outline, leave it out, change its colour or stitch direction, or move it earlier in the order.
- **See it in 3D before you sew.** Every stitch is drawn in a real hoop on a real fabric, with the thread's sheen.
  Press *Play* to watch it stitch out in order.
- **Get the file your machine reads.** Choose your machine from the list and Fembroider saves the right kind of
  file, ready to copy to its USB stick or card, with a printable colour list.
- **Save your work as a project.** Projects keep everything, including your pictures, and Fembroider saves as you go,
  so closing the window never loses your design.

## Three ways to stitch a picture

When you import a picture, the **Artwork** setting decides how it becomes stitches. Fembroider picks Flat art or Painting for you, and
you can switch at any time and compare. (When a picture looks like a drawing, it suggests Line art.)

- **Flat art** keeps every edge sharp. Best for logos, clipart and cartoons with clean areas of colour.
- **Painting** smooths brushwork and grain into calmer patches of colour. Best for paintings and photos: they sew
  much faster and with far fewer *jumps* (each time the machine stops, ties off and moves to a new spot).
- **Line art** follows the lines of a drawing with fine running stitch and turns shading into stitched hatching,
  in one thread. Best for ink drawings, engravings and woodcuts, and for star charts and other light-on-dark plates.

### Line art

Most embroidery programs fill everything in, which turns a pen drawing into a blotchy shape. Fembroider can follow
the lines instead.

![An engraving of Alice with the Gryphon. Left: the original. Middle: the usual filled-in result, a grey blur. Right: Fembroider's line art, which keeps the lines and the shading.](docs/images/line-art-tenniel.jpg)

<sub>John Tenniel's illustration for *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865) at 15 cm. Left: the original. Middle:
filled in the usual way, five threads and about 51 minutes. Right: Fembroider's line art, one thread and about 34 minutes.</sub>

Line art also works the other way round: white stars, comets and white-line prints on a dark ground. The light marks
are sewn in a light thread (stars become small tacked dots) and the dark ground is left to the fabric, so you sew them
on dark fabric.

![Two 19th-century astronomy plates: a comet and its nucleus, and Encke's Comet among stars. Each original is shown next to Fembroider's line-art version on dark fabric, with the comets and stars in pale thread.](docs/images/line-art-light-on-dark.jpg)

<sub>Plates from the British Library's digitised books: "Head and Nucleus of Comet, 1811" and "Encke's Comet", each at 10 cm.</sub>

All the artworks shown are in the public domain.

## Click to fine-tune

Fembroider does its best automatically, but you know your design better. **Click any shape** in the preview and a
round menu opens where you clicked, with everything you can change about that part. Drag a shape to move it.

![Three steps in dark mode: clicking the black outline of a strawberry sticker opens a round menu (Sew as, Direction, Colour, Sew order, Merge into, Leave out, Sticker edge, Layer); Sew as offers Auto, Satin, Flat fill, Lines and Outline; after choosing Lines the outline is a fine line](docs/images/pie-menu.jpg)

- **Sew as…** Auto, Satin, Flat fill, *Lines* (thin strokes become a single or triple running stitch along their
  middle, great for the linework of a drawing) or *Outline* (just the edge).
- **Leave out** a colour, for example a white border that the fabric can show instead. It stays as a faint dotted
  outline in the preview, so you can click it and bring it back.
- **Colour**, **Direction** of the stitches, **Sew order** and **Merge into** another colour (one less thread change).
- **Sticker edge** adds a satin or bean-stitch border round the whole design, like a die-cut sticker.

Made for designs drawn in Procreate and similar apps: export your sticker as a PNG with a transparent background
and import it.

![A strawberry sticker drawn as a PNG, stitched four ways: automatically; with the black linework sewn as lines and the white border left out; and with a bean-stitch edge](docs/images/sticker-edits.jpg)

Every change is undoable and saved with your project, and it stays put when you resize the design.

## Open designs you already have

Bought or free designs open as they are: **Import** a PES, DST, EXP, JEF, VP3, HUS, XXX or PEC file, or the zip it
came in (Fembroider lists the designs inside and picks the format with the most thread information). The design is
sewn exactly as it was digitized, every stitch kept.

![A strawberry design opened from a PES file, with the word Welcome added underneath, in dark mode. Clicking it opens the round menu with Colour, Sew order, Leave out and Layer](docs/images/open-design.jpg)

- **Combine** it with text, emoji or your own pictures in the same hoop, then save it for your machine. That also
  makes Fembroider a format converter (a PES becomes an EXP for a Bernina, for example).
- **Recolour** any thread, **leave a colour out**, or **sew it earlier or later**: click it in the preview, or use the
  list of colours in the panel.
- **Resize** by up to 15% either way. Finished stitches can't be scaled further without spoiling their density, so
  for a much bigger or smaller version, look for the design in that size.
- DST and EXP files don't store thread colours, so they open with placeholder colours for you to pick. An EXP opened
  together with its INF file keeps its colours.
- Working files from other programs (like Wilcom's .EMB) can only be opened in those programs. Look for a PES, DST,
  EXP or JEF version of the same design.

## Works with your machine

Fembroider knows 197 home and commercial machines and their hoops, from **Brother, Baby Lock, Janome, Elna, BERNINA,
bernette, PFAFF, Husqvarna Viking, Singer, Poolin, Tajima, Barudan, Happy, SWF, ZSK, Melco** and **Ricoma**. Pick
yours from the list at the top and the hoop sizes and file type follow. It checks that your design fits the hoop
before you save it.

## Getting started

1. **Download** the installer from the [Releases page](https://github.com/ifloofie/Fembroider/releases/latest)
   (the file called `Fembroider-Setup-….exe`) and open it.
2. Windows may show *“Windows protected your PC”*. That's because Fembroider is new and not yet signed by a
   company. Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
3. In Fembroider, pick your **machine** at the top, then add **Text**, an **Emoji** or **Import** a picture.
4. When you're happy with the 3D preview, go to **Export** and save the file onto your machine's USB stick or card.

## Questions

**Does it cost anything?** No. Fembroider is free and open source.

**Does it need the internet or an account?** No. Everything happens on your own computer, and nothing you make is
sent anywhere.

**Which file do I open on my machine?** Fembroider saves the type your machine reads (for example PES for Brother,
JEF for Janome, DST for commercial machines) and shows step-by-step instructions for your machine on the Export page.

**Where is my work saved?** Use *Project › Save* to keep a `.fembroider` file you can open again or share. On top of
that, Fembroider saves as you go and keeps earlier versions you can go back to (*Project › Version history*).

**My scan has page text or a border around the picture.** Open *Crop* under the picture's settings and press
*Find the picture*, or trim each side with the sliders.

**My picture came out messy.** Try making it bigger, choosing fewer colours, or switching *Artwork* between
*Flat art*, *Painting* and *Line art*. Hover over any setting in the app for a short explanation.
